Formula used

Base devices = ROUNDUP(Admissions × Eligibility × Length of treatment / 30 × Surge)

Maintenance buffer = 0 if Base devices = 0, otherwise MAX(1, ROUNDUP(Base devices × Maintenance %))

Devices needed = Base devices + Maintenance buffer

This estimate is based on empirically calibrated parameters and reflects average population-level patterns. Individual hospital case-mix may vary. The 95% confidence interval reflects statistical uncertainty in the underlying eligibility and length-of-treatment estimates.

Model parameters

LevelEligibility rateLength of treatmentSurge factorMaintenance buffer

Eligibility and length of treatment are empirically derived from patient-level data across 105 hospitals in 5 countries, using competing-risks methods to account for mortality and documentation gaps. Surge factor and maintenance buffer are fixed parameters (expert opinion and equipment audit data respectively) applied uniformly across levels.

Important considerations before use

  • Level-of-care labels follow the WHO classification framework for consistency across countries. Actual on-the-ground capacity at a given hospital may differ from what its label implies, and should be verified rather than assumed.
  • Estimates reflect current practice patterns, not a clinical guideline for ideal treatment duration.
  • Device cleaning, disinfection, and preparation time between patients is not yet factored into these estimates, and would increase the effective number of devices needed beyond what is shown here.
  • This tool has not yet been validated against independently measured device counts or usage in real hospitals.

Methodology

The formula above combines concurrent demand and surge into one rounded figure, then adds a maintenance buffer that is rounded separately and is always at least one device whenever there is any real demand. This avoids the buffer disappearing entirely for smaller hospitals, which a single combined rounding step would otherwise do, without introducing the larger inflation that comes from rounding every stage of the formula independently. Eligibility rate is calibrated per level of care using a hybrid approach that combines clinical criteria, empirically observed rates, and expert opinion, restricted to hospitals with plausible CPAP coverage (40-85%, or 50-85% for Level III). Length of treatment uses a competing-risks Restricted Mean Time Lost (RMTL) approach, which separates babies who died on CPAP from those successfully weaned, and treats undocumented discharge as censored rather than assuming an unrecorded wean date equals the discharge date.
