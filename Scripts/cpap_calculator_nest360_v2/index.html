Model parameters

LevelEligibility rateLength of treatmentSurge factorMaintenance buffer

Eligibility and length of treatment are empirically derived from patient-level data across 105 hospitals in 5 countries, using competing-risks methods to account for mortality and documentation gaps. Surge factor and maintenance buffer are fixed parameters (expert opinion and equipment audit data respectively) applied uniformly across levels.

Important considerations before use

  • Capacity to provide quality CPAP (staffing, infrastructure, oxygen supply, monitoring, etc.) must be assessed directly at each hospital before this estimate is used to inform procurement.
  • Level-of-care labels follow the WHO classification framework for consistency across countries. Actual on-the-ground capacity at a given hospital may differ from what its label implies, and should be verified rather than assumed.
  • Estimates reflect current practice patterns, not a clinical guideline for ideal treatment duration.
  • Device cleaning, disinfection, and preparation time between patients is not yet factored into these estimates, and would increase the effective number of devices needed beyond what is shown here.
  • This tool has not yet been validated against independently measured device counts or usage in real hospitals.

Methodology

Devices = ROUNDUP(Admissions × Eligibility rate × Length of treatment / 30 × Surge factor) + Maintenance buffer, where the maintenance buffer is rounded up separately and is always at least 1 whenever there is any real demand. Rounding the demand/surge portion and the maintenance buffer separately (rather than as one combined figure) ensures small hospitals still receive a meaningful equipment buffer, which a single combined rounding step could otherwise reduce to zero. Eligibility rate is calibrated per level of care using a hybrid approach that combines clinical criteria, empirically observed rates, and expert opinion, restricted to hospitals with plausible CPAP coverage (40-85%, or 50-85% for Level III). Length of treatment uses a competing-risks Restricted Mean Time Lost (RMTL) approach, which separates babies who died on CPAP from those successfully weaned, and treats undocumented discharge as censored rather than assuming an unrecorded wean date equals the discharge date.
